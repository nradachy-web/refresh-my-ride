# Royal Oak, Oakland County, MI: research for the town pages

Checked 2026-10-13. Every bullet is followed by the page it was confirmed on.
Numbers are here so a claim can be checked. The pages themselves do not print
statistics or weather numbers (see docs/CONTENT-GUIDE.md).

"Master plan" below means the City of Royal Oak Master Plan, 2012 amendment
(the 1999 plan as amended). It is the most recent full plan text that could be
read. The city adopted a new plan in 2025, but that PDF was too large to open,
so anything taken from the 2012 text is marked with its PDF page and should be
read as "true in 2012 and structural enough not to have changed".

## Where it is and what borders it

- Royal Oak is a city in Oakland County, described as an inner-ring suburb roughly 10 miles north of downtown Detroit.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- Neighbors: Birmingham, Troy and Clawson to the north. Madison Heights to the east. Oak Park, Ferndale, Pleasant Ridge and Hazel Park to the south. Beverly Hills, Southfield, Berkley and Huntington Woods to the west.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- The city's own description of its edges: most of the city is east of Woodward Avenue, I-696 is the southern boundary, I-75 is much of the eastern boundary, and the north boundary is "roughly defined by Fourteen Mile Road" with jogs to the nearest half mile roads. (Master plan, PDF page 99.)
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf
- The city is all land. Wikipedia gives a total area of about 11.8 square miles with 0.01 square miles or less of water. There is no lake in Royal Oak.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan

## The roads people drive

- I-75 runs north and south along the city's east side. I-696 runs east and west along the city's south side. Woodward Avenue (M-1) runs southeast to northwest along the west side. Five numbered east and west mile roads run through the city. Plain reading used on the tint page: on a road that runs southeast to northwest, someone who drives southeast in the morning and northwest in the evening has the low sun ahead or on the driver's side both ways. That is geometry worked out from the road's direction, not a sourced statement.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- 11 Mile Road "serves as main east-west thoroughfare through downtown Royal Oak". Livernois is known as Main Street in Royal Oak and Clawson. 14 Mile Road runs from Harper to Walled Lake with one brief interruption in Beverly Hills, which is west of Royal Oak, so it is continuous from Royal Oak east into Macomb County.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_and_freeways_in_metropolitan_Detroit
- "Being an older community, Royal Oak has a well-established grid-style street system." (Master plan, PDF page 96.)
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf
- I-696 exits on the Royal Oak stretch: exit 16 is M-1 (Woodward Avenue) and Main Street, exit 17 is Campbell Road and Hilton Road, exit 18 is I-75. Further east, exit 26 is M-97 (Groesbeck Highway). The freeway "follows the southern edge of the Detroit Zoo".
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_696
- North of 11 Mile Road, Woodward Avenue is the border between Berkley (west) and Royal Oak (east). North of 12 Mile Road it crosses fully into Royal Oak, and at 13 Mile Road there is a commercial district anchored by a shopping center and the hospital.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_(Michigan_highway)
- The downtown shopping district runs along Main Street and Washington Avenue. The Royal Oak Farmers Market is at the corner of 11 Mile Road and Troy Street.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan

## Who looks after the roads, and winter

- In Oakland County, MDOT has jurisdiction over about 230 miles of state highways (the I, M and US routes) and "contracts with the RCOC to maintain the state roads in Oakland County". Cities and villages are responsible for their own streets.
  https://www.oaklandcountymi.gov/government/about-oakland-county/roads-in-oakland-county
- The Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC) winter fact sheet for 2025 to 2026: RCOC maintains state highways for MDOT, "This includes I-75, I-696, I-96, M-59, Telegraph Road and Woodward Ave., among others." It uses an average of 64,000 tons of salt per winter. Its salt spreaders have pre-wetting devices "that spray salt brine on the salt as it is being spread". So salt and brine are both confirmed for I-696, I-75 and Woodward.
  https://rcocweb.org/DocumentCenter/View/10065/Winter-maintenance-preparations-fact-sheet?bidId=
- The City of Royal Oak plows and salts its own streets. "All major arterial streets such as 12 Mile Road are plowed and/or salted by special routes 24 hours a day as required." On other streets, "Crews salt intersections only where the majority of stopping and turning takes place." The whole city is plowed only in heavier snowfalls. (The page gives an inch figure. Do not print it: the guide bars weather numbers.) Only salt is mentioned for city streets, not brine.
  https://romi.gov/714/Snow-Emergency-Information
- In a declared snow emergency, on-street parking is prohibited except by special permit: "all vehicles on residential streets are to be removed or will be ticketed and/or towed", and "Vehicles are to remain off the streets until plow trucks have gone through the area."
  https://romi.gov/714/Snow-Emergency-Information

## Housing and parking

- Housing age: "These figures are indicative of an older suburb, where much of the housing was built prior to World War II. Mature trees in most neighborhoods reflect the older nature of these neighborhoods." The largest single decade for home building was the 1950s. (Master plan, PDF pages 88 and 89, from 2000 Census data. The percentages are not for the pages.)
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf
- Housing type: single family residential, defined as "single-family detached dwellings located on individual lots", "is the largest existing land use category in the city". Apartments and condominiums sit around the outskirts of downtown, south of downtown west of Main Street, north of 14 Mile Road and along Coolidge Highway. (Master plan, PDF page 100.)
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf
- House styles named in the plan: arts and crafts bungalows, American foursquares and Sears catalog houses in the older areas, cape cods and ranches in the postwar east side. The plan describes homes in the "older, more traditional neighborhoods" as having "parking to the side or rear of dwellings", with "detached garages in rear yards or attached garages on the sides". (Master plan, PDF pages 24, 105 and 106.) Plain reading: a typical older Royal Oak house has its garage at the side or back, reached by a driveway beside the house. Driveway width is not stated anywhere.
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf
- Downtown: "Royal Oak's downtown has developed as an entertainment and nightlife destination." "A number of large condominiums and lofts have been built in the area, increasing the density of the downtown population."
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- Downtown public parking is four city structures (Lafayette North Garage, Center Street Garage, Lafayette South Garage, 11 Mile Garage), surface lots with a 4 hour maximum stay, and on-street meters with a maximum stay of three hours. Structure payment is at pay stations on the first floor or by card at the exit.
  https://romi.gov/598/Parking-Permits
- The master plan describes downtown parking as "a combination of surface lots, structures, and on-street parking spaces" and says occupancy peaks in the evening, "reflecting the position of the downtown as an entertainment and restaurant district with strong night-time activity". (Master plan, PDF page 98.)
  https://greenwaycollab.com/Projects/SEMCOG_NoMo_Facilities_Inventory/Documents/Oakland/Plans/Royal%20Oak/FullCopyRoyalOakMasterPlan4-17-2012.pdf

## Trees, parks and what they put on and in a car

- "Extensive tree-planting has taken place since the 1930s, leading to the town being nicknamed The City of Trees." The Arbor Day Foundation has named Royal Oak a Tree City USA every year since 1976.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- Street trees "in the right-of-way (the lawn area between the sidewalk and the curb)" are public trees managed by the city, and a resident can ask for a free street tree to be planted there. Plain reading: the trees stand right beside where cars park at the curb.
  https://romi.gov/m/faq?cat=80
- Fall leaf collection: "The large number of trees in Royal Oak make the fall leaf collection a major task." Residents rake leaves into rows at the curb, and crews with leaf vacuums "cover the 212 miles of streets twice during the 6 week pick-up season". The city posts signs that "ask residents to park cars off the street" the day before pickup. Collection waits "until late October". (The 2026 schedule on romi.gov/380 runs November 2 to December 11. Time sensitive: do not print dates.)
  https://romi.gov/377/Leaf-Collection-Help
- The city lists 51 parks. ("Explore Our 51 Parks!")
  https://www.romi.gov/673/Listing-of-City-Parks
- Normandy Oaks Park (4234 Delemere) lists a splash pad, soccer field, playground, pavilion, picnic areas and trails.
  https://www.romi.gov/facilities/facility/details/Normandy-Oaks-Park-76
- Memorial Park is at 13 Mile and Woodward, on 24.19 acres, with baseball and softball diamonds, a football field, a tennis court and a playground.
  https://www.romi.gov/Facilities/Facility/Details/Memorial-Park-40
- Dogs: "Dogs with owner are welcome at City parks except for those parks with a sign posted No Dogs Allowed", and they must be leashed. Dogs are not allowed at Memorial Park except at special events.
  https://www.romi.gov/182/Pets-in-the-Parks
- Mark Twain Dog Park is a city dog park on N. Campbell Road, south of 14 Mile, that requires a membership application through the City Clerk.
  https://www.romi.gov/1378/Mark-Twain-Dog-Park

## Landmarks and employers

- Royal Oak "is also home to much of the Detroit Zoo, with portions extending into neighboring Huntington Woods", and the article calls it "a major regional tourist attraction". The zoo is at the Woodward Avenue and I-696 interchange (see the I-696 bullet above).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- Corewell Health William Beaumont University Hospital is at 3601 W 13 Mile Road, a 1,131 bed teaching hospital. The master plan called Beaumont "the largest employer in the City of Royal Oak" (2012, PDF page 92). Not rechecked for 2026, so the pages do not rank it.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corewell_Health_William_Beaumont_University_Hospital

## Car culture and events

- The Woodward Dream Cruise is "held each year on the third Saturday in August" on Woodward Avenue from Ferndale to Pontiac. Royal Oak is one of the communities on the route. The article gives about 1.5 million people and 40,000 vehicles. (The counts are not for the pages.)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward_Dream_Cruise
- "Royal Oak encompasses a major span of the Woodward Dream Cruise. The city sponsors ancillary events around the Cruise."
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Oak,_Michigan
- The city runs "Woodward Dream Cruise - Car Show in the Park" at Memorial Park, corner of Woodward Avenue and 13 Mile Road, on the Friday and Saturday of cruise weekend (the city page lists August 20 and 21, 2027).
  https://www.romi.gov/539/Special-Events
- Woodward's history with cars: "By 1958, the roadway was used for unofficial street racing", and automobile engineers street tested cars on Woodward between 8 Mile and Square Lake roads. All of M-1 is a Pure Michigan Byway and an All-American Road, listed as the Automotive Heritage Trail.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_(Michigan_highway)

## Drive

From docs/research/drive-times.json:

- measured_from: 203 S Troy St, Royal Oak, MI (City Hall, downtown)
- miles: 14.4
- minutes_no_traffic: 23
- On the pages: "about 14 miles from central Royal Oak" and "roughly 25 minutes without traffic".

Route: confirmed, two ways.

- Freeway, from downtown. I ran the same two points through the public OSRM router (OpenStreetMap data) and got 14.45 miles and 22.5 minutes, which matches the file, so the file's number is this route: 11 Mile Road east to the I-75 ramp at Stephenson Highway, a short hop south on I-75 to I-696 east, I-696 east about 7 miles to exit 26 (M-97, Groesbeck Highway), Groesbeck Highway north to 14 Mile Road, right (east) on 14 Mile Road, across Kelly Road, then Louise Ave on the left (north side). On the pages this is written as "I-696 east to Groesbeck Highway (M-97), north to 14 Mile Road, then east".
  https://router.project-osrm.org/route/v1/driving/-83.1415096,42.48876;-82.9260345,42.5403101?steps=true&overview=false
- Surface, from the north end of town. From 14 Mile Road and Main Street the router stays on 14 Mile Road east the whole way, then turns left onto Louise Ave: one road. That agrees with the Wikipedia description of 14 Mile Road as continuous from Harper to Beverly Hills. The mileage for this second route is not in drive-times.json, so the pages name the road and give no number for it.
  https://router.project-osrm.org/route/v1/driving/-83.1463,42.5334;-82.9260345,42.5403101?steps=true&overview=false
- Not checked by driving it. The router gives no-traffic times only. The pages say nothing about rush hour on I-696.

## Do not claim

Looked for and could not confirm. None of this goes on a page.

- A lake, beach, river, boat launch or Metropark in Royal Oak. None exists. The city is all land.
- Gravel or unpaved roads. The only mention found was in the 2012 master plan: two blocks of homes on Rochester Road reached by gravel frontage drives. Not rechecked, and not a pattern.
- That the city uses brine on its own streets. The city page says salt. Brine is confirmed only for the state highways RCOC maintains (I-696, I-75, Woodward).
- An overnight on-street parking ban, or which blocks need a residential parking permit. Nothing found on the city site. Only the snow emergency rule is confirmed.
- How wide Royal Oak driveways are, how many homes have a garage, or residential alleys. The plan describes garage position in older neighborhoods and nothing more.
- Whether a mobile detail is allowed in a city parking structure or at a metered space. Not addressed anywhere. The pages say a deck "may not" give enough room and steer those owners to valet.
- What kind of parking hospital staff or downtown workers use, and how long anyone's commute is. Not sourced, so the pages do not say where residents work.
- A sledding hill at Normandy Oaks Park. Reported in news coverage of the plan, not listed on the city's facility page.
- Royal Oak's current largest employer. The 2012 plan names Beaumont. Not rechecked.
- Dream Cruise car and crowd counts, zoo attendance, housing percentages and any snowfall or temperature number. They are in this file for checking only.
- The 2025 master plan's wording on housing and parking. The PDF could not be opened, so every master plan fact here is from the 2012 text.
- Traffic or travel time at rush hour on I-696, I-75 or 14 Mile Road.
- Royal Oak Township. The master plan lists it as a separate municipality from the City of Royal Oak. It is not what these pages are about.
- How many customers the shop has in Royal Oak, or any job done there.
