# Birmingham, Michigan (Oakland County): research

Fact sheet for the Refresh My Ride pages. Checked 2026-10-13. Every bullet is a plain fact followed by the page it was confirmed on. A local statement that is not in this file does not go on a page.

## The place

- Birmingham is a city in Oakland County with a total area of 4.80 square miles. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Michigan
- It is bordered by Bloomfield Hills on the northwest, Bloomfield Township on the west and north, Troy on the northeast, Royal Oak on the southeast and Southfield Township on the south. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Michigan
- The city describes itself as "approximately 20 miles north of downtown Detroit" and "in the southeastern portion of Oakland County". Source: https://bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/stats___facts.php
- The city's motto is "A Walkable Community", and the city describes "a pedestrian-friendly downtown with 1,500,000 square feet of retail space". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Michigan and https://bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/stats___facts.php

## Roads

- Woodward Avenue is state highway M-1 and is maintained by MDOT. North of 14 Mile Road in Birmingham, M-1 leaves the original route, which is now named Old Woodward Avenue, and runs east of it to bypass downtown. It returns to the original routing north of Maple (15 Mile) Road. The bypass opened in 1939 as Hunter Boulevard and was renamed Woodward Avenue in 1997. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_(Michigan_highway)
- The 1939 bypass was built for "drawing through traffic away from the busy Woodward Avenue-Maple Road intersection", so the original Woodward (now Old Woodward) and Maple Road cross in downtown. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_(Michigan_highway)
- All of M-1 is a Pure Michigan Byway and an All-American Road (the Automotive Heritage Trail). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_(Michigan_highway)
- Maple Road is 15 Mile Road and "forms main east-west thoroughfare through downtown Birmingham". 16 Mile Road is Big Beaver Road through Troy, Quarton Road west of Woodward ("minor local road west of Woodward") and Metropolitan Parkway through Macomb County. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_Road_System_(Detroit)
- 14 Mile Road "runs from Harper to Walled Lake, with brief interruption in Beverly Hills". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_Road_System_(Detroit)
- 14 Mile Road is at the south end of the city: the city says Lincoln Hills Golf Course "is located in the southwest portion of the city off 14 Mile Road". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/neighborhood_parks.php

## Winter and who treats the roads

- City streets are the city's job. The Department of Public Services says: "Major streets are salted first." Then, "if time and conditions permit, intersections of these streets and residential streets are then salted." The page refers to "salt or salt/chloride mixtures". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/snow_removal.php
- Top plowing priority goes to high traffic streets and intersections. Residential streets are plowed when conditions dictate. Downtown, snow is plowed to the center of the street and then hauled away. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/snow_removal.php
- A city ordinance prohibits parking on any street during a declared snow emergency or once snow accumulation reaches the ordinance threshold. Vehicles left on the street may be ticketed and towed. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/snow_removal.php
- The county road agency is the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC). Oakland County says MDOT has jurisdiction over state highways including "M-1 (Woodward Ave.)" and that "MDOT contracts with the RCOC to maintain the state roads in Oakland County". Cities and villages are responsible for their own streets. Source: https://www.oaklandcountymi.gov/government/about-oakland-county/roads-in-oakland-county
- RCOC salt trucks apply salt with liquid brine: "The salt is actually mixed with liquid brine as it comes out the back of the truck." (CBS Detroit, January 25, 2023.) Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/all-hands-on-deck-for-road-crews-in-oakland-county
- RCOC is the crew that plows and salts Woodward. An RCOC spokesman described "some of the state routes they plow" as "including Woodward Avenue". The report is old (CBS Detroit and WWJ, December 3, 2010), so it supports who does the work and nothing about how much salt goes down today. Added by the fact check, 2026-10-13. Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/fewer-plows-less-salt-this-season-in-oakland-county/

## Street surfaces (gravel under a seal)

- "The Department of Public Services maintains nearly 26 miles of unimproved roadways through cape seal surface treatment." The city defines an unimproved road as "a gravel road, with or without curbs, that has been maintained with chip or cape seal to provide a relatively smooth and dust-free driving surface". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/streets/cape_seal.php
- Cape seal is made by "rolling stone chips into a layer of liquid asphalt emulsion, followed later by an application of a slurry micro-surface". The city calls the result "little more than a surface crust on a gravel road". Re-treatment "can be expected every 7-10 years". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/streets/cape_seal.php
- "Prior to 1930, when many of Birmingham's neighborhoods were subdivided and opened for development, local streets were built as gravel roads with little if any provision for storm drainage." Also: "Issues related to standing water, drainage, grade, and profile cannot be remedied through cape seal maintenance." Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/streets/cape_seal.php

## Parking

- "The City of Birmingham owns and operates five parking structures providing over 3,500 parking spaces for public use in the Central Business District." They are the Chester, N. Old Woodward, Park, Peabody and Pierce garages. "The first 2 hours are free in all five of the City-owned parking decks." Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php
- The city has about 1,200 parking meters ("one of Birmingham's 1,200 parking meters"), with on street meter parking at two hourly rates. Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php
- Monthly permits are sold for each of the five city structures. Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php
- Residential parking permits exist "only for residents living on streets designated for residential permit parking". Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php
- Getting into a city structure means reaching a machine from the driver's seat. The city's section on the structure equipment says visitors "Pull ticket at entry, scan ticket at exit" or use a debit or credit card ("insert or tap to pay (no cash)"), and monthly pass holders "Scan your parking pass, or use the TIBA Mobile Access App". The same section mentions gate arms. Added by the fact check, 2026-10-13. Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php
- The city also has municipal surface lots, so structures and meters are not the only public parking: "The City's parking policy applies to any public street that has individually marked parking spaces and all municipal lots." The page does not say where the 1,200 meters are. Added by the fact check, 2026-10-13. Source: http://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/police_department/parking_information/index.php

## Trees and leaves

- Public trees, including those in street rights-of-way, "are managed by the City's Department of Public Services". They "are pruned routinely as part of the City's block trimming cycle", and "the City of Birmingham plants about 200 public trees each year". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/forestry___environment/forestry_faq.php
- Fall leaf collection on curbed streets: "You are asked to rake the leaves into the street the weekend prior to your zone pickup window", and "leaves are to be in the street immediately next to the curb". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/government/departments/dps/leaf_collection.php
- "Residents living on improved streets enjoy the benefit of street-side leaf pickup during the months of October and November." Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/streets/cape_seal.php
- Raking into the street is for curbed streets only. "There are two types of collection depending on whether your street is curbed or uncurbed." On uncurbed streets leaves go out in paper yard waste bags or decal containers on trash day. The October and November months come from the cape seal page above. The leaf collection page gives its dates only on a map image. Pages must tie the raked leaves to a curb, never to every street. Added by the fact check, 2026-10-13. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/government/departments/dps/leaf_collection.php

## Parks, water and trails

- "Twenty-six parks with over 230 acres cover more than 10% of the City's total acreage." The city lists "two miles of trails along the Rouge River" and two 9-hole city golf courses. Source: https://bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/stats___facts.php
- "Quarton Lake, located on the city's west side, is fed by the Rouge River." Quarton Lake Park "surrounds the lake almost entirely". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/quarton_lake.php
- "The Rouge River Trail System can be accessed at multiple points throughout the City including a trailhead at Booth Park downtown." Source: https://downtownbirmingham.com/visit/parks-and-recreation
- DO NOT USE the Booth Park trailhead on a page until the park reopens. The city's Booth Park page is headed "Booth Park closed for construction until summer 2027" and says: "The closure includes the playground, the sledding hill and the Booth Park entrance to/exit from the Rouge River chip trail." The fact check removed the trailhead from the detailing page on 2026-10-13. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/booth_park.php
- The same page gives the trail a name that says what it is made of: "the Rouge River chip trail". That is the only city wording found for the surface of the river trail. Added by the fact check, 2026-10-13. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/booth_park.php
- Linden Park has "access to the Rouge River Trail Corridor from Shirley and Hawthorne". Manor Park is undeveloped open space with a wood chip trail. Springdale Park and Golf Course sits "along the Rouge River". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/neighborhood_parks.php
- The city's off-leash dog area is at Lincoln Hills Golf Course, off 14 Mile Road: "In the fall of 2010, an off-leash dog area was re-establish at Lincoln Hills Golf Course." Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/neighborhood_parks.php
- The dog park's own page places it "at the southeast edge of the Lincoln Hills Golf Course, 2666 West Fourteen Mile Road" and says it "is open every day". No closure notice on 2026-10-13. Added by the fact check. Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/lincoln_hills_dog_park.php
- The Birmingham Ice Sports Arena is at 2300 East Lincoln on the Kenning Park property. It has a regulation-sized rink, is home to the Birmingham Unified hockey team (Seaholm and Groves high schools) and the Birmingham Hockey Association youth program, and high school home games "are played from November through February". Source: https://www.bhamgov.org/government/departments/dps/ice_sports_arena.php

## Landmarks and schools

- Shain Park is the city's main commons, the Birmingham Museum is at Maple and Southfield Roads, and the Birmingham City School District includes Seaholm High School. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Michigan and https://www.bhamgov.org/about_birmingham/city_departments/public_services/parks___recreation/parks___facilities/neighborhood_parks.php

## Car culture

- The Woodward Dream Cruise is held on the third Saturday in August and "runs along Woodward Avenue ... from Ferndale to Pontiac". Birmingham is one of the sponsoring communities, was on the first route in 1995, and was one of the nine participating Oakland County communities in 2026. Dates: August 15, 2026 and August 21, 2027. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward_Dream_Cruise and https://oaklandcountyblog.com/2026/08/12/the-2026-woodward-dream-cruise/
- The Birmingham Cruise Event is held "along South Old Woodward Avenue" on Dream Cruise Saturday (August 15, 2026, 9am to 5pm) and shows classic cars. It returns Saturday, August 21, 2027. Sources: https://downtownbirmingham.com/do/birmingham-cruise-event-1 and https://downtownbirmingham.com/cruiseevent

## Drive

- From docs/research/drive-times.json: measured from 151 Martin St, Birmingham, MI. 17.2 miles. 32 minutes without traffic. On the pages this is written the way the content guide table gives it: "about 17 miles from central Birmingham" and "roughly 30 minutes without traffic".
- Route confirmed: yes. A routing engine query from 151 Martin St to 33115 Louise Ave returned two main-road routes of the same length in time. Source: https://router.project-osrm.org/route/v1/driving/-83.2155271,42.5455782;-82.9260345,42.5403101?steps=true&overview=false&alternatives=3
  - Route A (17.3 miles, 31.7 minutes, the route the drive-times numbers match): Maple Road, Adams Road, then east on Big Beaver Road, which becomes Metropolitan Parkway, then right on Utica Road, left on 14 Mile Road, left on Louise Avenue.
  - Route B (15.7 miles, 31.8 minutes): Woodward Avenue (M-1) south to 14 Mile Road, then east on 14 Mile Road for about 14 miles without a turn, then left on Louise Avenue. This agrees with the Mile Road System entry above: 14 Mile Road is continuous from Harper west past Woodward.
- Pages describe Route B, the simple one: Woodward south to 14 Mile Road, then east on 14 Mile. The shop is on the north side of 14 Mile, just east of Kelly Road, so it is on the left for a driver coming from Birmingham. Only the town hub also mentions Route A.
- Caution: Route B is about a mile and a half shorter than the "about 17 miles" the pages state. The mileage on the pages follows the content guide table and the fastest route, not the 14 Mile route.
- Shop coordinates used for the query (33115 Louise Avenue, Clinton Township): 42.5403101, -82.9260345. Source: https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=33115+Louise+Ave,+Clinton+Township,+MI+48035&format=json&limit=3

## Do not claim

Looked for and could not confirm. None of this is on the pages.

- The housing mix: the share of single family homes, how many have driveways or garages, how many people live in downtown condos or apartments. Only real estate sites gave figures. The pages speak to the reader ("if you have a driveway") and do not describe the town's housing.
- An overnight street parking ban. Found only the snow emergency rule.
- That the city itself uses liquid brine. The city page says "salt or salt/chloride mixtures". Brine is confirmed for the county road commission only.
- Which named streets are on the city's "major streets" list. The page does not list them.
- Dust, loose stone or stone chip damage from the unimproved streets. The city says cape seal gives a "relatively smooth and dust-free" surface. No source ties these streets to paint damage.
- "About 30% of streets are unimproved". One undated TV story quotes it. The pages use the city's "nearly 26 miles" only.
- Quarton Lake details beyond the city's two sentences: a dam, a mill pond, the trail surface, fishing. Only blogs and trail apps describe them.
- The surface of the Rouge River trails beyond the city's own name for it, "the Rouge River chip trail", and the wood chip trail at Manor Park. No source says the whole two miles is chip.
- Booth Park as a way onto the trail while the park is closed for construction (until summer 2027 on the city's page).
- A count of street trees, a tree canopy figure, or Tree City USA status. The state page that lists it would not load.
- How many cars are in the Birmingham Cruise Event. Sources disagree ("nearly 200" and "thousands").
- That 14 Mile Road is the exact city limit along its whole length. Sources support "the south end of the city" only.
- That every one of the five city structures has the ticket and card equipment. The city describes it for "the parking structures" without naming each one. See the Parking section for what is confirmed.
- That the unimproved streets commonly hold standing water. The city says only that they were built with little if any provision for storm drainage and that cape seal cannot remedy standing water. Pages keep it conditional ("where water stands").
- That public parking downtown is only structures and meters. The city also has municipal lots.
- That the 1,200 meters are all downtown. The city gives the count for Birmingham and no location.
- That every level of a parking structure is out of the sun. Pages say "a covered level".
- Whether the Amtrak stop described on Wikipedia is still in Birmingham.
- Large employers, commuting patterns, or a mall inside the city limits.
- Angled or parallel parking on Old Woodward today.
- Road Commission for Oakland County jurisdiction over any road inside Birmingham other than state highway M-1.

## Fact check, 2026-10-13

An independent pass re-opened the sources above and compared them with the five Birmingham pages.

- Confirmed as written, on the page cited: the 4.80 square miles, the borders and the motto (Wikipedia), the M-1 bypass, Old Woodward and the Maple Road crossing (Wikipedia M-1), the three mile road entries (Wikipedia Mile Road System), salting order and the snow rules (city), the MDOT contract with RCOC (Oakland County), salt mixed with brine (CBS Detroit, 2023), nearly 26 miles of unimproved roadways and the cape seal quotes (city), five structures, 1,200 meters and residential permits (city), public trees and about 200 planted a year (city), leaves at the curb on curbed streets (city), two miles of Rouge River trails (city), Quarton Lake (city), the Booth Park trailhead as downtownbirmingham.com words it (closed for now, see the next bullet), Manor Park, Linden Park, Springdale and the Lincoln Hills off-leash dog area (city), the ice arena address, Kenning Park and the November through February home games (city), the third Saturday in August (Wikipedia), the Birmingham Cruise Event "Along South Old Woodward Avenue" with "classic cars of all makes and models" (downtownbirmingham.com).
- Found and fixed on the pages: the Booth Park trailhead (the park and its trail entrance are closed for construction until summer 2027); "Parking downtown means one of five city structures or one of the city's roughly 1,200 meters" (the city also has municipal lots, and gives no location for the meters); "streets that hold water" stated as fact (the city does not say how common standing water is); "a car is mostly out of the sun" inside a structure (now "a covered level"); "about 17 miles" joined by a colon to the 14 Mile Road route, which is 15.7 miles (the figure now stands in its own sentence).
- Still current on 2026-10-13: the ice arena page lists a fall and winter open skate schedule "effective September 8-January 9, 2027", and the parks page carries no closure notice for the Lincoln Hills off-leash dog area.
- Route re-run on the same routing engine URL. Route A: 27,783.6 meters (17.3 miles), 1,900.9 seconds. Steps: Maple Road, Adams Road, right on Big Beaver Road, straight on as it becomes Metropolitan Parkway, right on Utica Road, left on 14 Mile Road, left on Louise Avenue. Route B: 25,239.0 meters (15.7 miles), 1,907.7 seconds. Steps: Woodward Avenue (M-1) south, a turnaround on Woodward at 14 Mile Road, then 14 Mile Road east for about 14 miles with no turn, left on Louise Avenue. Both match the Drive section.
- 14 Mile Road at the south end of the city rests on two sources: the city's "southwest portion of the city off 14 Mile Road" for Lincoln Hills, and the M-1 article's "North of 14 Mile Road in Birmingham". No source was found that calls 14 Mile Road the city limit, so pages say "the south end of the city" and nothing stronger.
- Not re-checked because no page uses them: the 2 hours free quote, the nine communities in 2026, the museum and school district line.
- Web search was not available for part of this pass. Every item above was confirmed by opening the cited page directly.
