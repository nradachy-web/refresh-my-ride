# Rochester Hills, Michigan (Oakland County): fact sheet

Researched 2026-10-13 for the Refresh My Ride town pages. Each bullet is a plain fact followed by the page it was confirmed on. Where a fact was read through a search excerpt and not the page itself, the bullet says so.

## Roads and borders

- Rochester Hills is in Oakland County. It is bordered on the north by Oakland Charter Township along Dutton Road, on the south by Troy along South Boulevard, on the east by Shelby Township in Macomb County along Dequindre Road, and on the west by Auburn Hills along part of Adams Road. The city of Rochester sits against part of its east side.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills,_Michigan
- M-59 crosses the city as a freeway. Its exits in Rochester Hills are Adams Road (the westbound exit is by way of Hamlin Road), Crooks Road (exit 44) and Rochester Road, M-150 (exit 46). The freeway runs from just east of downtown Pontiac to Utica, where it becomes Hall Road, a surface boulevard, in Macomb County.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-59_(Michigan_highway)
- M-59 runs east and west. M-150 runs north and south.
  https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills
  Fact check 2026-10-13: Wikipedia's M-59 article also calls it an east and west trunkline, and the freeway's mapped line (OpenStreetMap, from Squirrel Road to Dequindre Road) runs about 7.3 miles east for 1.2 miles south, a bearing of about 100 degrees. It has one short diagonal stretch between Livernois Road and Rochester Road. The pages say only that it runs east and west and that the low sun is ahead of you eastbound in the morning and westbound in the evening. They do not say the road "lines up with" sunrise or sunset, which is only true near the equinoxes.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-59_(Michigan_highway)
- M-59 is a state highway that the Road Commission for Oakland County maintains. Oakland County's roads page says MDOT has jurisdiction over about 230 miles of state highways in the county, names M-59, I-75 and I-696 as examples, and says "MDOT contracts with the RCOC to maintain the state roads in Oakland County". Added by the fact checker 2026-10-13. This is the source for the pages saying county crews treat M-59.
  https://www.oaklandcountymi.gov/government/about-oakland-county/roads-in-oakland-county
- M-150 is Rochester Road. It runs about 4.8 miles from the M-59 interchange in Rochester Hills north to Tienken Road in Rochester, where it is one of the main streets of downtown Rochester. MDOT maintains it.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-150_(Michigan_highway)
- The old route of M-59 along Auburn Road in Rochester Hills is still maintained by MDOT.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-59_(Michigan_highway)
- The city says some major roads inside the city limits are county or MDOT roads, and that those are maintained by the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC) or by the city.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/public_services_engineering/roads.php
- Public transportation in Rochester Hills is described as weak, and a car is described as the practical way to get around.
  https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills

## Winter road treatment

- The city's Department of Public Services clears ice and snow from nearly 300 miles of roads and 99 miles of pathways. City crews do not maintain roads that belong to RCOC, MDOT or private owners.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/public_services_engineering/winter_road_maintenance.php
- The city plows and salts 53 miles of major roads around the clock as needed. That figure includes 5 miles of county-owned Livernois Road.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/public_services_engineering/winter_road_maintenance.php
- The city also plows and salts its 241 miles of local roads: during DPS business hours when snowfall is 4 inches or less, and around the clock as needed when it is more. The page says "plowed and salted" for local roads in both cases. Priority order is major roads and emergency, school bus and industrial routes first, residential streets second, pathways third.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/public_services_engineering/winter_road_maintenance.php
- RCOC plows state highways as well as county roads in Oakland County. It uses salt primarily on paved roads and sets its priorities by traffic volume and speed.
  https://www.rcocweb.org/160/Snow-Plowing
- RCOC describes its crews as plowing and salting during storms.
  https://www.rcocweb.org/259/Winter-Roads
- RCOC crews pre-treat roads by spraying liquid brine ahead of a storm (news report dated February 11, 2025, quoting the road commission).
  https://fox2detroit.com/news/oakland-county-prepares-roads-ahead-wednesday-winter-storm

## Land, hills and housing

- The city was Avon Township until November 20, 1984, when it became the City of Rochester Hills. The name was chosen partly for the rolling hills in the area. Elevation runs from 690 feet in the southeast to 1,032 feet in the northwest.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills,_Michigan
  Fact check 2026-10-13: the USGS elevation service agrees with the direction of the slope. It returns about 678 feet at South Boulevard and Dequindre Road (southeast corner), about 797 feet at City Hall and about 970 feet at Dutton Road and Adams Road (northwest corner). The pages give no elevation numbers.
  https://epqs.nationalmap.gov/v1/json?x=-83.0935&y=42.6215&units=Feet&wkid=4326
- Single family residential is the largest land use category in the city: 8,939.1 acres, 42.4 percent of the land, and 20,392 of 22,273 parcels (Oakland County 2025 Land Use Statistics, Community 70, City of Rochester Hills). The next largest category is road right-of-way at 15.8 percent. The fact checker downloaded the PDF and read the table directly on 2026-10-13. Used on the pages only as "single family homes are the largest land use", with no number.
  https://www.oakgov.com/home/showpublisheddocument/20339/638719299182900000

## Trees and green space

- The city has a Forestry Unit that divides the city into seven tree management districts and does most of its street tree work from November through March. A Street Tree Ordinance requires a city permit to remove a tree in the right-of-way. A Tree Conservation Ordinance requires a permit to remove most private trees 6 inches or more in diameter, with an exception for occupied one-family parcels under one acre.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trees.php
- The city owns eight Green Space properties bought under a millage voters approved in 2005. Several border the Clinton River. The city's descriptions include steep slopes, woodland, an old-growth tree canopy on one property and mature cottonwood, ash, elm and maple on another.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/greenspace.php

## Parks, trails and water

- The Clinton River Trail runs 4.5 miles through Rochester Hills. It is part of a 16 mile trail on an abandoned rail line that connects to the West Bloomfield Trail and the Macomb Orchard Trail.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/clintonrivertrail.php
- The Paint Creek Trail is an 8.9 mile linear park converted from the former Penn Central Railroad. 1.5 miles of it are in Rochester Hills. It has a limestone fine surface.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/paintcreektrail.php
- Bloomer Park, 345 John R Road, has hiking and mountain bike trails and a sledding and tobogganing hill.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/bloomeryatesparks.php
- Spencer Park, 3701 John R Road, is a 113 acre park with a 38 acre lake, a beach, fishing and ice skating. The city page currently lists swimming as closed until the 2027 season.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/spencerpark.php
- Innovation Hills, 2800 W Hamlin Road, is 110 acres with a 3 acre playground, about 1 mile of paved trail and boardwalk, ponds, access to the Clinton River and a kayak landing. Pets are allowed on a 6 foot leash.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/innovationhills.php
- Borden Park, 1400 E Hamlin Road, is a 143 acre sports complex with 4 lighted softball and baseball fields, 5 full size soccer fields plus smaller ones, pickleball, basketball and tennis courts, roller hockey rinks and 30 acres of wooded wetland. Pets are allowed on a 6 foot leash.
  https://www.rochesterhills.org/departments/parks_and_natural_resources/trailsandparks/borden_park.php

## Employers, schools and landmarks

- Oakland University's 1,443 acre campus spans Auburn Hills and Rochester Hills. Most of the land area of the campus is in Rochester Hills. Many of its students commute from surrounding areas.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_University
  Fact check 2026-10-13: the same Wikipedia note goes on to say "most of its academic and residential buildings are located in Auburn Hills". A boundary lookup agrees: the academic core near the Oakland Center falls inside Auburn Hills, and Meadow Brook Hall and the east side of the grounds fall inside Rochester Hills. So the lots most students and staff park in are in Auburn Hills. The tint page said "Most of Oakland University's campus land is in Rochester Hills" as the lead in to a point about parked cars, which is true of the acreage but misleading about the parking. It now says the campus "spans Rochester Hills and Auburn Hills".
- Meadow Brook Hall, 350 Estate Drive, Rochester Hills, stands on the Oakland University grounds. It was built between 1926 and 1929 by Matilda Dodge Wilson, heiress to the Dodge automaker fortune, and was named a National Historic Landmark in 2012.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadow_Brook_Hall
- The Village of Rochester Hills is an open-air lifestyle center at the northeast corner of Walton Boulevard and Adams Road. It opened in 2002 and replaced a small enclosed mall.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_of_Rochester_Hills
  Fact check 2026-10-13: Wikipedia's own words are "lifestyle center", not "open-air". A second source uses the phrase: a construction report dated January 18, 2002 says "the Village of Rochester Hills will be an open air mall" with "Main Street" style stores and nearby parking, at Adams and Walton roads.
  https://michiganbuildingtrades.org/newspaper/muddy-rochester-village-takes-shape
- FANUC America's Americas headquarters is at 3900 W Hamlin Road, Rochester Hills. (Not used on the pages.)
  https://fanucamerica.com/about-us/locations/headquarters-rochester-hills-michigan
- Rochester Community Schools serve most of the city. Rochester Adams, Rochester High and Stoney Creek High are in the city. Parts of the city are in the Avondale School District. (Not used on the pages.)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Hills,_Michigan

  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadow_Brook_Hall
  CORRECTED by the fact checker 2026-10-13. This bullet first said "every August", following Wikipedia's "held annually during August". That is not true of every year: the last one at Meadow Brook, the 32nd annual, was held on Sunday, July 25, 2010. The paint correction page said "every August" and now says "every summer". The 1979 start and 2010 end are consistent with a 32nd annual show in 2010.
  https://oaklandpostonline.com/8163/news/concours-returns-to-meadow-brook-hall

## Drive

From docs/research/drive-times.json:

- Measured from: 1000 Rochester Hills Dr, Rochester Hills, MI (City Hall)
- Miles: 18.7
- Minutes without traffic: 31
- On the pages: "about 19 miles from central Rochester Hills" and "roughly 30 minutes without traffic".

Route: route not confirmed.

What I could establish: a routing engine (OSRM, on OpenStreetMap data, queried from City Hall to a point at 14 Mile Road and Kelly Road) returns 18.9 miles and about 32 minutes, which matches the numbers above. Its path is West Avon Road, Rochester Road (M-150) south, M-59 east, off at Van Dyke Avenue in Utica, Utica Road southeast, then 14 Mile Road east to Kelly Road.
https://router.project-osrm.org/route/v1/driving/-83.1582745,42.6633666;-82.9262,42.5358?overview=false&steps=true

Fact check 2026-10-13: the same engine, run again from City Hall, returned 18.9 miles and 32 minutes by the same roads. From the far northwest corner of the city (Dutton Road and Adams Road) it returned 23.7 miles and 38 minutes, and from Auburn Road and John R Road in the southeast 14.1 miles and 25 minutes. So the whole city sits inside the "about 30 to 40 minutes" the shop gives for mobile and valet, but the northwest corner is near the outer edge of it.

That is four roads and three turns, not a simple main-road run like Sterling Heights has on 14 Mile Road. It is one engine's no-traffic pick, not checked against a second map and not checked with the shop, and a driver starting from the south or east end of the city would likely go a different way. So the pages give the distance, the time and where the shop sits (north side of 14 Mile Road, just east of Kelly Road) and do not describe a route. The pages do say the shop is across the county line in Macomb County, which follows from Dequindre Road being both the city's east border and the county line.

## Do not claim

- Gravel or unpaved roads in Rochester Hills. I looked on the city's Roads and Winter Road Maintenance pages, the RCOC gravel road pages and in local news. RCOC has about 755 miles of gravel road countywide, but nothing I found puts a mileage or a road name inside Rochester Hills.
- Tree City USA status. A search excerpt of a 2026 Michigan DNR release appeared to list Rochester Hills, but the page returned an error when opened and an older DNR bulletin did not list it.
- A tree canopy percentage, a street tree count, or "tree lined streets" as a description of the whole city. The pages only say the city protects street trees by permit and has a forestry unit.
- The surface of the Clinton River Trail inside Rochester Hills. Sources describe the whole trail as a mix of asphalt and gravel or crushed stone and none break it down by city. Only the Paint Creek Trail's limestone surface is confirmed.
- People swimming at Spencer Park now. The city page lists swimming as closed until the 2027 season.
- Yates Cider Mill as a Rochester Hills landmark. Wikipedia places it in Rochester, a historical marker listing places it in Rochester Hills. Left off the pages.
- Parking decks, street parking, condo share or how many residents use a garage. No source. The only parking structure I found is on the Oakland University campus, and I did not confirm which city it stands in.
- Whether the lots at The Village of Rochester Hills or Oakland University are shaded or open. The pages say "if" the car is parked in an open lot.
- Brine on city streets. The city page says salted. Brine is confirmed only for RCOC crews.
- Which named roads besides Livernois are county roads. (Who maintains M-59 is now sourced: see the Oakland County roads page under Roads and borders. What is still not sourced is brine on M-59 by name. The brine report is about road commission crews in general, so the pages say the commission's crews spray brine ahead of storms and do not say "M-59 is brined".)
- That the Concours d'Elegance was held "every August". The 2010 show was on July 25. Say "every summer".
- That Oakland University's parking lots are in Rochester Hills. Most of its academic and residential buildings are in Auburn Hills. Say the campus spans both cities.
- That every street in the city is salted. The city page covers its 53 miles of major roads and 241 miles of local roads. Private roads, county roads and state roads are not the city's, so the pages say "local streets" or "local roads" and not "every street".
- Any Huron-Clinton Metropark inside the city. I did not find one and did not use one.
- Dates for 2027 car events, or that the Cars and Coffee meets will repeat.
- Employee counts, traffic counts, income or any other statistic about residents.
